Mission

Safer skies, today and tomorrow.

Challenge

Threats change faster than fixed systems.

As drones become more accessible, risks to people and critical infrastructure are growing. Interceptor drones offer an effective response. Mission requirements vary widely, and rapidly advancing technology can make fixed solutions obsolete. To remain effective, interceptors must be flexible and evolve just as quickly.

Mission

A platform instead of a single airframe.

Boreas is building a digital design and manufacturing platform that enables the rapid development of tailored interceptor drones. Design automation adapts systems to new requirements, while additive manufacturing enables flexible production.

  1. Input

    Requirements

    The user defines what the mission needs: range, speed, interception time and cost.

  2. Pipeline

    Design automation

    Requirements become technical parameters and a concrete design, coupling aerodynamics, structures, propulsion, electronics and control.

  3. Output

    Additive manufacturing

    The design is produced flexibly, assembled simply and commissioned with a low threshold for testing.

Ethics Statement

Ethical standards

Our primary product is the engineering pipeline, not the drone. The interceptor is a defined case study to evaluate the method, and the method stays transferable to other platforms such as capture and net drones.

The underlying engineering is dual-use. We address this through responsible system design, information security and publication practice, together with the ETH Ethics Commission. Weapons, explosive payloads and autonomous target selection remain outside the project.

The scientific methodology may be made transparent. The immediate automation of a deployable design is not published without further review and appropriate restrictions.
Ethics Statement v2.2 · 29.09.2026 · Preliminary
Read the seven guidelines
  1. Transferability. The pipeline remains applicable to other platforms, including capture and net drones.
  2. No weapons. Autonomous target selection, engagement without human authorisation, weapons and explosive payloads remain outside the project.
  3. Responsible publication. Methodology and general findings may be published openly; directly deployable implementations undergo separate review.
  4. Information security. Sensitive technical information and other enabling materials are protected against unauthorised access or disclosure.
  5. Responsible collaboration. External collaboration is possible if information sharing stays proportionate to the jointly defined scope.
  6. Project-associated personnel. Everyone with substantial access to sensitive information follows the Statement and relevant ETH rules.
  7. Human responsibility. The pipeline supports engineering decisions but makes no operational or societal decisions concerning actual interception.

After the Focus Project, the results transition to the pd|z laboratory. Prof. Mirko Meboldt endorses the Statement and its continued application.
